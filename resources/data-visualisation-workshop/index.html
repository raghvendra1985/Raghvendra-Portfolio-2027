Data Visualisation Workshop

Choose and test an honest visual explanation of data.

For design students, early-career designers and practitioners building a more deliberate process. Use individually or in a facilitated workshop. The example is illustrative; adapt the method to your context.

Work through the activity

01 · Define the question

Write what the audience needs to compare, locate or understand. State where the data came from, the period it covers and its limitations.

02 · Inspect the data

Identify units, missing values, categories, sample size and uncertainty. Record transformations and keep a path back to the source.

03 · Choose an encoding

Match the question to position, length, area, colour or text. Compare two chart approaches and explain why one supports the task better.

04 · Audit honesty and access

Check scales, baselines, labels, units, colour contrast and reliance on colour alone. Provide a text takeaway and a data table or equivalent accessible alternative.

05 · Test comprehension

Ask someone to describe the key comparison and its uncertainty. Record misreadings, revise one encoding or annotation, and state what the chart cannot establish.

Worked example

Illustrative workshop example: a small synthetic dataset compares travel times across modes. A bar chart with units and a table communicates the comparison; the designer does not imply the data represents a whole city.
