Growing With Kid

Small moments. Big yaadein.

Visit live site→
Role
Solo founder
Timeline
2020–present
Platform
Web · print or phone
AudienceObjectiveDecisionsProductFramesOutcome

Context

Growing With Kid started in 2020 around one evening question for Indian families. After a long day, with so much parenting advice to sort through, what could we actually do together tonight? It began as writing, practical guides and conversations. The live site is that job, made small enough to use: activities, little stories, rituals and keepsakes for a grown-up and child, ages 3–9, in 5–20 minutes.

Build story

Problem
Indian families had plenty of parenting advice and not enough of a clear next thing to do together on an ordinary evening.
Bet
One clear job — something a grown-up and child can do tonight — beats another feed of advice.
Build
Screen-light activities, eight character guides, a free three-day sampler, a keepsake journal, and separate paths for schools and partners. Bolo Buddy is one of the guides.
Ship
Live on the web. Print the pages or follow the steps on a phone. The monthly membership is marked in prelaunch.
Learn
Constraint stayed the editor. Software-first experiments without trust felt like theatre, so the product leads with a page to print or a few steps on a phone.

Audience

Primary: Indian families with children ages 3–9. A grown-up stays in the activity. The same site also speaks to teachers and to brand partners, with the family moment still in the middle.

Design objective

Help a family find one little thing they can do together today, and make it easy to come back for another. Screen-light. Materials people already have. Most ideas take 5–20 minutes. Print them, or follow along on a phone. No app for the guided steps.

Three decisions

  1. 01

    Define one job: something to do together tonight, not a catalogue of parenting advice. The founder note on the site still uses the 2020 question.

  2. 02

    Lead with something a family can hold. The free mini pack is a PDF or a few steps on a phone, after software-first experiments felt like theatre without trust. The Friday note and the WhatsApp group are the way back, not a second product.

  3. 03

    Keep it small on purpose: ages 3–9, 5–20 minutes, eight guides, materials already at home. The failed assumption was that more modules would equal more retention.

What the live site offers

These are the offers on growingwithkid.com. Prices below are the planned figures printed on the membership page, not sales.

  • Free mini pack

    A Week of Little Yaadein. Three guided activities to print or follow on a phone: a time capsule, a bedtime ritual, and a paper creation. The page describes a planned seven-day edition that would add four more and a printable keepsake.

  • Monthly membership, in prelaunch

    Four activities a month, printable pages, steps on a phone, ages 3–9. The page asks for a launch-update email. Planned pilot price: ₹199 per month. Planned options, which the page says will be reviewed after the pilot: ₹549 every three months and ₹1,999 per year. Billing has not started.

  • Friday note and WhatsApp

    One activity to try together, by email, or a WhatsApp group for parents and caregivers. The WhatsApp page describes the plan as one prompt a week and a weekend check-in, and asks people to keep children’s names and photos private.

  • Schools

    Ready-to-run 5–20 minute activities for Indian classrooms, ages 3–9. A teacher sampler (a tiny time capsule, a picture conversation, and a feelings prompt) and an enquiry for a four-week pilot.

  • Partners

    Sponsored packs, characters, events and a content series. The page says partner case studies are not published yet. The examples are labelled sample concepts, not completed client work. The fit test on the page: useful for a real family moment, suitable for ages 3–9, a grown-up stays in it.

  • Eight guides

    Memory Mouse, Craft Fox, Bolo Buddy, Grandpa Bear, Book Bird, Nature Deer, Science Elephant and Puzzle Pal. Each one is a way into a kind of day. Bolo Buddy is one guide here; the storytelling product has its own case study.

Outcome

business

A live family product, with the membership still ahead

Growing With Kid is on the web. Families can take the three-day sampler. Schools and partners can write in. The monthly membership is marked in prelaunch, and the page says billing starts only after someone chooses to subscribe once the pilot opens. The site does not publish usage or revenue figures.

operational

The job stayed small

The surface is wider than the early newsletter — activities, printables, guides, a journal, three audiences — and the constraint is the same one the founder note names. Ten minutes, materials already at home, a grown-up and a child. Depth over noise, now as something to do rather than something to read.
